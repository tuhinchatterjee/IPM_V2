# Retail Scorecard Validation

What the module is, what it refuses to do, and where each part lives.

Everything below is generated by and checked against code in
`backend/scorecard/`. Where a number appears, it comes from the built
demonstration universe and a test asserts it.

---

## 1. What this module is

A governed workspace for validating two retail scorecards — an **Application**
scorecard scored at the point of decision, and a **Behavioral** scorecard
scored monthly on the live book. It computes the standard model-validation
statistics, derives a validation opinion from governed policy, and produces a
CBUAE MMS/MMG-aligned validation report with an evidence workbook behind it.

**What it is not.** It is not a rating model, not an IRB PD model, not an
IFRS 9 ECL engine, and it does not provide regulatory certification or a legal
compliance opinion. "CBUAE MMS/MMG-aligned" is a claim about the report's
section list, not about a regulator having approved anything.

**The data is synthetic.** Every row, dataset version and catalogue entry
carries `origin = SYNTHETIC_DEMO`. It describes no real customer and no real
bank's book, and it is not client data.

---

## 2. The rule the module is built around

> Never calculate actual against predicted on a cohort whose performance
> window has not closed.

This is §7, and it is the reason for most of the module's shape.

The universe holds **31 months** (2023-01 to 2025-07). The performance window
is **12 months** and outcomes are observable to **2026-01**, so **25 months
have matured** and the last **six (2025-02 to 2025-07) have not**. Those six
exist deliberately: a universe where every month happened to be matured would
leave the refusal implemented, unit-tested and unreachable — the month picker's
"stability only" marker would never appear and no report could show the
difference between *no defaults* and *no outcome yet*.

On an open month the module reports stability, distribution and implementation
metrics — none of which needs an outcome — and states the month the window
closes in. It never returns zero.

`backend/scorecard/synthetic.py`: `matured()`, `window_closes()`,
`latest_matured()`.

## 3. The three separable questions

| Question | Metric family | Needs an outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Does the score **rank** risk correctly? | AUC, Gini, KS, gains | Yes |
| Is the predicted **level** of risk right? | ODR vs PD, Brier, log loss, calibration slope, guarded MAPE | Yes |
| Has the **population** moved from the one the model was built on? | Score PSI, per-variable CSI, missing/unseen bin rates | No |

These are kept apart everywhere — in the engine, in the corpus families, in
the report sections — because they get merged in conversation and a system
that merged them could not tell whether it had answered the question asked.

A fourth question, **implementation replication**, asks whether production
computes the same score from the same equation. It needs no outcome either.

## 5. What the module refuses

Each of these is a check in `backend/scorecard/critical.py`, run against the
real engine, and each fails in the flattering direction — which is why it is
asserted rather than watched for.

- An outcome metric on an open performance window.
- A score mapping with no declared direction; a metric that assumes one.
- Good and bad reversed in the outcome column.
- A term silently treated as zero when its column is absent.
- An unregistered model version.
- Application data scored with the behavioural equation.
- A raw column where a Weight of Evidence column belongs.
- PSI against a baseline other than the declared one.
- A variable's CSI reported as the score's PSI.
- KS or Gini computed by a route that disagrees with AUC.
- A predicted probability outside [0, 1].
- Production and the registry running different equations.
- Validation months inside the development fit.
- A discrimination statistic on a sample with one outcome class.
- A segment too small to carry the metric, ranked anyway.
- MAPE on a band whose observed rate is at or near zero.
- Models compared on different populations or periods.
- A report figure that does not match the dashboard it came from.
- A claim that CreditProbe certifies regulatory compliance.
- A candidate model activated by the act that proposed it.
- Activation that retires by the wrong key, leaving two scorecards live.

## 6. Governance

**Limits carry their source.** All 15 seeded limits are `DEMO POLICY`. The
0.10 and 0.25 PSI cut-offs are a scorecard convention, not a regulatory
threshold, and nothing in the module presents them as one. A metric with no
approved limit reads **NO APPROVED LIMIT**, which is not a pass and is not the
same as **NOT MEASURED** — three states, three colours.

**Candidates never activate.** A proposed equation becomes a `CANDIDATE`
version beside the active one. `ACTIVE` is reachable only from `APPROVED`, and
proposing requires a wider permission than approving.

**Demographic fields are not scoreable.** `applicant_age` and
`marital_status` are held for fairness monitoring; an equation referencing one
is refused by the validator, not by convention.
